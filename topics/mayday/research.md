# Mayday fact check
- Coined 1923 by Frederick Stanley Mockford, senior radio officer, Croydon Airport (London); much traffic Croydon–Le Bourget (Paris); from French "m'aider" (venez m'aider). (Wikipedia "Mayday"; Britannica; Smithsonian Air & Space)
- Voice radio replacing Morse/wireless telegraphy in 1920s aviation; "SOS" spoken is hard to distinguish (S vs F). (Wikipedia "Mayday")
- Adopted as the international voice distress signal by the International Radiotelegraph Convention, Washington, 1927.
- Said three times so it isn't confused with a message about a mayday call. (ICAO / USCG procedures)
- "Pan-pan" = urgency (not immediately life-threatening), from French "panne" (breakdown). (Wikipedia "Pan-pan")
