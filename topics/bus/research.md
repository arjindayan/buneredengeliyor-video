# School bus yellow fact check
- April 1939 conference at Teachers College, Columbia University, organized by Prof. Frank W. Cyr; week-long; officials from each of the then 48 states, bus chassis/body makers, paint experts (DuPont, Pittsburgh Paints); set 44 national standards incl. body color. (Wikipedia "School bus yellow", "School bus")
- Color originally "National School Bus Chrome", now "National School Bus Glossy Yellow" (US and Canada); Federal Standard 595a color 13432 (National Bureau of Standards).
- Stated rationale (Cyr): black lettering most legible on yellow-orange, esp. dawn/dusk; conspicuous. Reported, not independently verified; script says "the reason given".
- Early buses: "kid hacks" repurposed farm wagons, open to the weather; purpose-built school car 1892 (Wayne Works). Pre-1939 colors not documented, script avoids claiming them. "no national standard" inferred from the 1939 standardization.
- Not claimed: peripheral-vision folk claims, other countries' practice.
