# Goodbye fact check
- "goodbye" 1570s, contraction of "God be with ye" (godbwye, God be wy you, God b'w'y); "good" substituted for "God" by analogy with good day, good night, good morning. (Etymonline "goodbye"; Merriam-Webster; OED)
- Earliest cited use: Gabriel Harvey, letter of 1573: "To requite your gallonde of godbwyes, I regive you a pottle of howdyes." (Etymonline; OED)
- French adieu < à Dieu "to God"; Spanish adiós < a Dios "to God". (Etymonline "adieu"; RAE)
- "over four hundred years old": 1573 → 453 years.
