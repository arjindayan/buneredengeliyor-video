# Knots fact check
- Knot = 1 nautical mile/hour = exactly 1.852 km/h; nautical mile 1852 m. "Knots per hour" is incorrect usage. (Wikipedia "Knot (unit)")
- Chip log: wooden quadrant weighted to float upright, on a reel line with knots at equal intervals, thrown over the stern; one sailor times with a sand glass, another counts knots passing. (Wikipedia "Chip log")
- Spacing 47 ft 3 in with 28 s glass is the accepted pairing (47 ft 3 in = 20.25 in/s -> 1.8517 km/h); older ships used ~8 fathoms (48 ft) and 28/14/30 s glasses. Script says "forty-seven feet" and "about half a minute" (spacing "evenly spaced", "matched to nautical mile and glass").
- Earliest printed description: William Bourne, A Regiment for the Sea, 1574. Unit name first appearance not documented in sources, not claimed.
- Not claimed: Dutchman's log / Crescêncio attribution (disputed).
