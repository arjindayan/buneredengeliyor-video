# Why 60 minutes? Fact check
- Sumerian/Babylonian sexagesimal (base-60) system, 3rd–2nd millennium BC, written in cuneiform on clay with a reed stylus. (Britannica "sexagesimal numeral system"; Scientific American "Why is a minute divided into 60 seconds…")
- 60 has 12 divisors: 1,2,3,4,5,6,10,12,15,20,30,60. 100 is not divisible by 3.
- Knuckle counting (thumb over 12 phalanges × 5 fingers of other hand = 60) is a popular hypothesis, not proven → script says "one popular theory".
- Egyptians: 12 daytime + 12 night-time hours (decans, sundials); hours were seasonal/unequal. (Sci Am; Britannica "hour")
- Hipparchus (2nd c. BC) proposed equinoctial (equal) hours; Greek astronomers used Babylonian sexagesimal fractions (Ptolemy, Almagest).
- Latin "pars minuta prima" (first small part, 1/60) → minute; "pars minuta secunda" (second small part, 1/3600) → second. (Etymonline "minute", "second")
- French Republican decimal time: decreed Nov 1793 (10 h/day, 100 min/h, 100 s/min); mandatory use suspended 7 April 1795. (Britannica "French republican calendar"; Wikipedia "Decimal time")
