# Cheese: verified claims
- Earliest daguerreotype portraits needed long exposures (Getty: 15+ min at first, soon ~1 min; TIFO: seconds by mid-1840s). Exposure was a factor, not the main reason: social norms were (Getty).
- Broad grins associated with lewdness, drunkenness, lower classes; serious people kept mouths closed (Getty, citing Angus Trumble / Nicholas Jeeves).
- London daguerreotypist Richard Beard told sitters to "say prunes" for a tight mouth (Getty).
- Kodak marketed photography as joyous; $1 Brownie 1900 (TIFO; Getty on Kodak ads).
- 1943 Big Spring Herald (Texas): ambassador Joseph E. Davies, "Just say 'Cheese,' It's an automatic smile" (TIFO, one of the earliest print references).
Sources: getty.edu/news/why-no-smile-photographs-history-education-question-answer ; todayifoundout.com/?p=20119 ; Kotchemidova, "Why We Say Cheese" (2005).
Gemini second opinion: no false claims; flagged Beard as not provably the originator (script doesn't claim that).
