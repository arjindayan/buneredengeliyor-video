# Clockwise fact check
- Northern-hemisphere sundial shadow moves W → N → E, i.e. clockwise seen from above; early European mechanical clocks (late 1200s–1300s) copied that motion. Sources: Science Focus "Why do clocks go clockwise?"; Country Life "Curious Questions: Why do clocks go clockwise?"; Hodinkee "Why clocks run clockwise"; Today I Found Out (2016). Standard accepted explanation; no serious rival theory.
- Southern hemisphere: sun crosses the northern sky, shadow turns anticlockwise. (Brian Koberlein "Widdershins"; Science Focus)
- Paolo Uccello painted the Florence Cathedral clock face, 1443; 24-hour dial, hand runs anticlockwise. (Opera di Santa Maria del Fiore magazine; WeTheItalians)
- Jewish Town Hall, Prague: lower clock with Hebrew numerals, hands run anticlockwise (Hebrew reads right to left). (Lonely Planet; World Jewish Travel)
- Premise holds. Wording "secretly copying a shadow" is framing.
