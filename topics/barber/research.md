# Barber pole fact check
- Medieval/early modern European barber-surgeons did bloodletting, tooth extraction, minor surgery (Britannica "barber pole"; Smithsonian Magazine "Why barber poles are red, white and blue").
- Bloodletting rested on humoral theory: illness from imbalance/excess of blood (Britannica "bloodletting").
- Patient gripped a staff/rod to make veins stand out; staff → pole (Britannica; Smithsonian).
- Red = blood, white = bandages; bandages washed and hung to dry, twisting around the pole — widely repeated, partly folklore → script says "The story goes".
- Brass basin on top: represented the basin for leeches / collected blood (Britannica; Smithsonian).
- 1540: Company of Barber-Surgeons (London); 1745: surgeons separated → Company of Surgeons (Act of Parliament under George II). (Royal College of Surgeons history)
- Blue in US poles: commonly attributed to the national flag; not documented → script says "most likely".
