# Clinking glasses fact check
- Poison-sloshing origin: popular claim with no documentary support (Snopes "Clinking glasses"; Paul Dickson, "Toasts"; Smithsonian/History.com note it as legend). Script: "zero evidence", "never found an old source".
- "Toast" sense: 1600s custom of putting spiced toast in wine/ale (Shakespeare, Merry Wives c.1602 "put a toast in't"); by c.1700 "toast" = person drunk to, as if they flavoured the drink; "toast of the town". (Etymonline "toast")
- Clinking reason unknown; most cited theory = complete the five senses (hearing); presented as theory. (History.com; Dickson)
- "group hug" line is framing, not a factual claim.
